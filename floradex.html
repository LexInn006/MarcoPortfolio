FloraDex · AR Experience

Turning a picture book into something you can hold and spin

FloraDex scans pages straight out of a children's picture book and brings fruits and vegetables to life as interactive 3D models — built to make learning more visual, engaging, and memorable for elementary school students.

2025
Duration

Background

Picture books are pretty, but passive

Today's children's picture books tend to look great, but they're fairly static — a page you look at once and move on from. The team wanted to pair that visual appeal with something interactive, aimed at building a more AR-based learning medium that's engaging for elementary school students, so material actually sticks.

Our application

Scan the page, meet the object

FloraDex is a mobile app that lets students scan an image straight from their picture book to bring up a 3D object, live, on their phone screen — starting with fruits and vegetables, chosen as the most approachable place to begin.

Research

A brainstorm, then a direct observation

The AR-scanning theme itself was set by the course lecturer, but the specific idea — scanning children's picture books — came from the team's own brainstorm. The team didn't look at other AR education apps beforehand; the idea came purely from thinking through what felt monotonous about picture books as they exist today. That discussion then led into direct observation to confirm the instinct. The team started with fruits and vegetables specifically because it was the most approachable starting point.

The app itself isn't scoped to a specific age range — it's built to be simple enough for any child who can follow along, and just as usable for the parent sitting next to them.

Development process

From needs analysis to a working AR scan

  1. Needs analysis
    Identifying what an interactive, AR-based learning medium needs to offer elementary school students.
  2. UI/UX design
    Designing an interface simple enough to navigate — covering the home, history, scan, and settings pages.
  3. Build in Unity
    Building the app in Unity 6, integrating Vuforia for image-target detection, and bringing in the 3D fruit assets.
  4. Testing & iteration
    Testing the AR scan feature and refining it — including reworking the History page so it felt and worked better.
Ideation

Everything planned, shipped

Every feature the team planned made it into the final build — nothing had to be cut. The one real challenge was the History feature, which fell to Marco to build. It was new territory for him, tracking and storing each scanned item along with its name and educational info, but it came together successfully by the end.

Testing & iteration

Solid concept, UI still a work in progress

Overall, the concept held up well in testing. The one honest gap was the UI — it worked, but the team felt it still had room to be developed further. The History page in particular went through a round of refinement to get its functionality and feel right.

Final thoughts

Learned, mostly

This project was Marco's deepest dive into Unity scripting so far — genuinely new territory, and where most of the learning happened. There's no specific next step planned for FloraDex right now, and no single moment Marco points to as the proudest part of it — it was more about the process of learning Unity scripting hands-on than any one result.
