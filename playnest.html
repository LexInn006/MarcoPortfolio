PlayNest · UI/UX Concept

Fewer screens, more growth — without asking parents for more time

An SDG-4-aligned concept that blends curated learning videos with bookable, offline activities, designed for parents who want their kids to grow and have fun safely, but don't have the time to plan it themselves.

Year
2025
Duration

The problem

Busy parents, overstimulated kids

Parents who want their kids to grow and have fun safely often don't have the time to plan it. Kids end up handed a gadget to stay occupied, and even with screen-time limits set, those limits aren't consistently enforced — leaving kids overusing overstimulating content that isn't suited for their age.

The solution

Curated learning, plus real offline play

PlayNest combines curated learning videos with structured, bookable offline activities — rotated every period like a normal service booking. Content is curated in-house, so parents get a trustworthy alternative to open-ended gadget time, without having to plan any of it themselves.

Background

Where the idea came from

PlayNest was built for a Creativity and Innovation course, where a team of four was challenged to develop an SDG-aligned business concept for children, plus a working prototype. The team chose SDG 4: Quality Education.

The starting observation was personal: Marco's own nieces and nephews are often given gadgets by busy parents to keep them occupied. Even with screen-time limits parents had set, those limits weren't consistently enforced because of busy schedules — so the kids ended up overusing gadgets and watching overstimulating content unsuited for their age. That pattern became the problem PlayNest set out to solve.

The team's concept summary — benefits, core functions, and target market, mapped against SDG 4.
Research

A quick look at what already exists

The team looked at YouTube Kids as the closest existing alternative, and found it lacking on two fronts: it's video-only, which the team felt wasn't enough on its own for a child's growth, and it's a public platform — meaning there's always a chance a video slips through that isn't actually appropriate for young children. That gap, between passive video-watching and genuine structured growth, shaped PlayNest's offline-activity component.

The team also interviewed a family with a young child. One insight stood out: a parent raised concern about how uncontrolled — even addictive — their child's screen time had become. That single comment became the direct inspiration for PlayNest's offline activity feature.

Empathy map built from the interview — parents' fears (fraud, safety, unmet expectations) directly shaped PlayNest's trust-building features.
Define

Designing around a real parent

The persona came directly from the family the team interviewed — not a fictionalized composite. Silvia Piyo, 30, runs her own business and is raising a 2-year-old. Between running the business and being present for her child, she's constantly relearning how to manage her time without sacrificing either.

How might we give busy parents a way to support their child's growth and play, without asking for more of their time?
Ideation & Design

Building what mattered, keeping it simple

Rather than exploring a wide set of features and cutting them down, the team went straight for what felt most essential: curated video learning, bookable offline activities, and a subscription structure that refreshes every month. On the interface, the goal was to keep things simple enough that parents wouldn't feel overwhelmed, while still feeling playful and a little cartoonish — approachable for both a parent scrolling quickly and a child looking over their shoulder.

Value proposition mapping — pains like fraud risk and safety concerns were countered directly with a satisfaction guarantee and a points system.
Testing & iteration

Presented, not stress-tested

The Figma prototype was presented to a course lecturer and a business expert. Both responded positively, specifically noting that while video-based learning concepts are common, PlayNest's offline activity component was rare. No further revisions came out of that session.

Final thoughts

Looking back

Learned

This was Marco's first UI/UX project, and the first stretch of it felt unfamiliar. That discomfort pushed real learning — exploring independently, researching other products' UI/UX, and, as the one leading the project, learning to set deadlines, keep everyone accountable, split tasks by strength, and get everything done on time.

Next steps

No concrete next step for now — but given the chance to continue, the goal would be building out the full application so it can genuinely help parents who want to support their child's growth.

Proudest of

How satisfying the final result turned out — especially the UI — the positive feedback from the business expert, and how evenly the team's workload was shared.
